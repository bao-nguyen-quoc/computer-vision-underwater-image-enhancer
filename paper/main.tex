\begin{abstract}
    Underwater image enhancement (UIE) is often applied before object detection. Recent studies indicate that using one enhancement method for every image does not raise detection accuracy. We investigate a selective alternative on the RUOD benchmark with a YOLO11n detector. A compact ResNet18 selector picks, for each image, among the raw input, SCNet, and Semi-UIR. Its training targets stem from an out-of-fold protocol, preventing any detector from scoring images seen during training. The selector matches the mAP of raw images by leaving most images unchanged. We also introduce a control in which an oracle chooses between a raw image and its horizontal reflection; this geometric control yields an mAP gain comparable to oracles formed over enhanced candidates, indicating that detector sensitivity to spatial shifts contributes to the oracle margin. Fusing predictions of four views with weighted boxes fusion gives a modest gain over raw images. Finally, training detectors on both raw and enhanced versions matches compute-matched raw baselines on clean images while providing higher mAP across 12 unseen degradation conditions. In our experiments, enhancement proved more effective as multi-domain training data for robustness than as a per-image choice at test time.

    \keywords{Underwater image enhancement \and Underwater object detection \and Selective enhancement \and YOLO11 \and RUOD \and UIEB.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Cameras on autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs) record images affected by wavelength-dependent light absorption and scattering~\cite{fuRethinkingGeneralUnderwater2023,liUnderwaterImageEnhancement2019}. Red light attenuates within shallow depths. Consequently, captured scenes frequently exhibit low contrast, green or blue color casts, soft boundaries, and forward-scattering haze.

Deep learning architectures for underwater image enhancement (UIE), such as SCNet~\cite{fuUnderwaterImageEnhancement2022} and Semi-UIR~\cite{huangContrastiveSemisupervisedLearning2023}, deliver high PSNR and SSIM against reference images. Conventional practice inserts such models as an upstream preprocessing step, assuming that cleaner visual quality translates into higher detection accuracy.

Awad et al.~\cite{awadRevisitingUnderwaterImage2026} demonstrated that this assumption does not hold universally. Across several detectors, applying a single enhancer to all images reduced average detection accuracy relative to raw images, despite individual frames showing improvement. On the RUOD benchmark~\cite{fuRethinkingGeneralUnderwater2023}, they computed an idealized mixed-set upper bound by selecting per image the best among ten candidates (the raw image and nine enhanced variants) using ground-truth annotations. That oracle lifted the mean per-image mAP from 0.68 to 0.77 (+0.09).

This theoretical upper bound motivates two questions. First, can an automated selector choose the winning version directly from raw pixels without ground-truth annotations? Second, how much of the oracle margin stems from genuine optical restoration rather than detector variance across different representations of the same scene?

We examine these questions with YOLO11n~\cite{jocherUltralyticsYOLO2024} on the 14{,}000-image RUOD benchmark. Candidate versions for each image include the raw input, SCNet, and Semi-UIR. Our findings include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate an out-of-fold labeling protocol with a margin $\delta = 0.01$. A ResNet18 selector~\cite{heDeepResidualLearning2016} operating on $224 \times 224$ raw inputs requires 7.7\,ms per frame.
    \item On 4{,}200 test images, the thresholded selector yields 0.6159 mAP@0.5:0.95 (+3.2\% of the oracle gap closed) against 0.6155 for raw images. It keeps the raw input for 85.5\% of queries. Uniform SCNet gives 0.6129, Semi-UIR gives 0.6084, and random selection gives 0.6120.
    \item In a control experiment without enhancement, an oracle choosing between a raw image and its horizontal reflection gains +0.0207 per-image mAP. This matches 144\% of the +0.0144 gain achieved by the oracle over raw and SCNet candidates.
    \item Fusing detections across four views via weighted boxes fusion (WBF)~\cite{solovyevWeightedBoxesFusion2021} gives +0.0023 mAP@0.5:0.95 on the complete test set, with a 95\% confidence interval of $[+0.0014, +0.0051]$ strictly above zero.
    \item Multi-version training across raw and enhanced variants increases raw-image mAP@0.5:0.95 from 0.5990 to 0.6155 over a 50-epoch baseline, matching an epoch-matched 150-epoch raw detector (0.6150). It further provides higher detection mAP across 12 unseen degradation conditions, although it receives three times more image presentations during training.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Underwater Image Enhancement}

SCNet~\cite{fuUnderwaterImageEnhancement2022} targets variable water conditions. At several scales of a U-Net, it decorrelates spatial activations through instance whitening and standardizes channel statistics, ensuring that intermediate features depend less on specific water types.

Semi-UIR~\cite{huangContrastiveSemisupervisedLearning2023} mitigates paired training data scarcity via a mean-teacher framework with contrastive regularization. A reliable memory bank stores pseudo-labels filtered by a no-reference quality metric. At inference time, an attention-guided illumination modulation network (AIMnet) processes multi-scale Gaussian luminance representations.

Both models were benchmarked on UIEB~\cite{liUnderwaterImageEnhancement2019} using PSNR and SSIM. These metrics assess fidelity against reference photographs rather than downstream detection utility.

\subsection{Enhancement for Underwater Object Detection}

Fu et al.~\cite{fuRethinkingGeneralUnderwater2023} introduced RUOD for underwater detection, observing variable interactions between common enhancers and detector architectures. Luo et al.~\cite{luoMASYOLOv11ImprovedUnderwater2025} proposed MAS-YOLOv11 with multi-scale attention heads for cluttered underwater environments. Huang et al.~\cite{huangAMIEODAdaptiveMultiExperts2026} developed AMIEOD to route low-illumination inputs dynamically across specialized enhancement modules.

Awad et al.~\cite{awadRevisitingUnderwaterImage2026} compared nine enhancers across modern detectors. Uniform application of any enhancer failed to outperform raw inputs over the full test distribution. They introduced a per-image mixed-set oracle, though it relies on test-time annotations and separately trained domain detectors.

\subsection{Box Fusion and Test-Time Augmentation}

Test-time augmentation combines detections from transformed input views. Standard non-maximum suppression (NMS) retains one bounding box per cluster and discards overlapping candidates. Weighted boxes fusion (WBF)~\cite{solovyevWeightedBoxesFusion2021} instead clusters overlapping boxes and computes confidence-weighted coordinate averages.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{System Overview}

Let $x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ denote an underwater RGB frame. We employ two public pre-trained enhancers with author-released weights: SCNet ($E_{\mathrm{SC}}$) and Semi-UIR ($E_{\mathrm{SU}}$). Candidate representations comprise
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{V}(x) = \{\, v_0, v_1, v_2 \,\} = \{\, x,\; E_{\mathrm{SC}}(x),\; E_{\mathrm{SU}}(x) \,\}.
\end{equation}
A selector $f_\theta$ processes raw frame $x$ and outputs index $\hat{k} \in \{0, 1, 2\}$. Chosen candidate $v_{\hat{k}}$ is then supplied to fine-tuned YOLO11n detector $D$. Fig.~\ref{fig:sample_enhancement} shows a sample underwater image before and after enhancement with Semi-UIR and SCNet. The overall workflow, encompassing out-of-fold label generation and selective inference, is illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}.

\subsection{Enhancers and Processing Pipeline}
\label{sec:method-enh}

Both enhancers run at native resolution ($640$\,px long edge) with released model parameters.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SCNet:} We isolate the enhancement decoder branch, excluding the auxiliary VGG16 perceptual network. Inputs are reflect-padded to multiples of 8, and outputs are cropped and quantized to $[0, 255]$.
    \item \textbf{Semi-UIR:} Luminance map $L(x)$ is computed by convolving the luminance channel with Gaussian kernels at scales $\sigma \in \{15, 60, 90\}$, averaging logarithmic responses, and normalizing to $[0, 255]$. Inputs and luminance maps are reflect-padded to multiples of 16. Processing averages 2\,s per frame.
\end{itemize}
Outputs are stored as JPEG files ($\text{quality} = 95$) sharing original annotations.

\subsection{Out-of-Fold (OOF) Selector Labeling}
\label{sec:method-labels}

Evaluating a detector on its training data inflates scores, biasing target labels. We employ a three-fold out-of-fold protocol:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The 9{,}800 RUOD training frames are partitioned into three folds $\mathcal{F}_0, \mathcal{F}_1, \mathcal{F}_2$ with seed 0 (MD5 hash: \texttt{ea44b385539c}).
    \item For fold $k \in \{0, 1, 2\}$, a YOLO11n detector is trained for 30 epochs on all three candidate versions of the remaining folds ($\approx 19{,}600$ images).
    \item Checkpoint \texttt{last.pt} evaluates candidates of held-out fold $\mathcal{F}_k$ ($\text{conf} = 0.001$, $\text{IoU} = 0.7$).
    \item Using \texttt{pycocotools}, per-image mAP@0.5:0.95 values $m_{i,0}, m_{i,1}, m_{i,2}$ are recorded. Unannotated frames receive 0.
\end{enumerate}
With margin $\delta = 0.01$, target label $y_i$ is assigned as
\begin{equation}
    y_i =
    \begin{cases}
        0                    & \text{if } \max_{k \in \{1, 2\}} m_{i,k} - m_{i,0} < \delta, \\
        \arg\max_{k} m_{i,k} & \text{otherwise}.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Selector Architecture and Decision Rules}

The selector is a ResNet18~\cite{heDeepResidualLearning2016} backbone pretrained on ImageNet, with a three-way linear head. It processes raw inputs resized to $224 \times 224 \times 3$ (7.7\,ms on an NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPU). We train it via two objectives:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Class-weighted cross-entropy (\texttt{ce}):}
          \begin{equation}
              \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ce}} = -\sum_{c=0}^2 w_c \cdot \mathbb{I}(y = c) \log p_c,
          \end{equation}
          where $w_c$ denotes inverse class frequency.
    \item \textbf{mAP regression (\texttt{reg}):} the network predicts mAP values $(\hat{m}_0, \hat{m}_1, \hat{m}_2)$ supervised by Smooth L1 loss.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Decision Threshold ($\tau$)}
To prevent low-confidence enhancement calls, we select $k^* = \arg\max_{k \in \{1, 2\}} p_k$ only if $p_{k^*} - p_0 > \tau$; otherwise, the raw image is retained. Under regression, the condition is $\hat{m}_{k^*} - \hat{m}_0 > \tau$.

\subsubsection{Validation Selection Criterion}
Always predicting raw achieves 58.2\% accuracy on training labels and 62.4\% on test labels. Simple classification accuracy is therefore an uninformative objective. Model selection and threshold tuning rely on chosen-mAP across a held-out 20\% validation subset (1{,}960 images):
\begin{equation}
    \text{chosen-mAP} = \frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{val}}} \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{val}}} m_{i, \hat{y}_i}.
\end{equation}
Tuned thresholds are $\tau = 0.625$ for \texttt{ce} and $\tau = 0.040$ for \texttt{reg}.

\subsection{Evaluation by Composing Saved Predictions}

Evaluation on 4{,}200 RUOD test images proceeds in four stages:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A single detector (\texttt{full.pt}) trains for 50 epochs on all three versions of 9{,}800 training images ($29{,}400$ frames total, 10{,}732\,s training time).
    \item Predictions across all versions are precomputed and cached. Each policy is scored by selecting corresponding predictions and invoking \texttt{pycocotools}.
    \item Paired bootstrap resampling (1{,}000 iterations) computes 95\% confidence intervals and empirical p-values $p_{\text{boot}}(\Delta \le 0)$ for per-image mAP differences against raw baseline.
    \item Closed gap percentage is computed as
          \begin{equation}
              G = \frac{\mathrm{mAP}_{\mathrm{policy}} - \mathrm{mAP}_{\mathrm{orig}}}{\mathrm{mAP}_{\mathrm{oracle}} - \mathrm{mAP}_{\mathrm{orig}}} \times 100\%.
          \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Oracle Control with Horizontal Flip}
\label{sec:method-noise}

To examine whether oracle gains reflect enhancer processing or detector variance, we introduce a geometric control via horizontal reflection $T_{\mathrm{flip}}$. For input $x$, bounding boxes predicted on $T_{\mathrm{flip}}(x)$ are mapped to original coordinates via inverse transform $T_{\mathrm{flip}}^{-1}$. Flipped frames preserve exact color and contrast distributions. Differences between $m_{i, \mathrm{orig}}$ and $m_{i, \mathrm{flip}}$ thus reflect detector spatial sensitivity and NMS clustering. We define
\begin{equation}
    \text{Oracle}_{\{\mathrm{orig}, \mathrm{flip}\}}(i) = \max \left( m_{i, \mathrm{orig}},\; m_{i, \mathrm{flip}} \right).
\end{equation}
If this control yields gains comparable to enhancement oracles, a portion of the apparent upper bound can be attributed to detector sensitivity rather than optical improvement.

\subsection{Multi-View Weighted Boxes Fusion}
\label{sec:method-wbf}

We fuse candidate detections across raw, flipped, SCNet, and Semi-UIR inputs using WBF~\cite{solovyevWeightedBoxesFusion2021} with fixed parameters: $\text{IoU}_{\mathrm{thr}} = 0.6$, $\text{skip\_box\_thr} = 0.001$, and maximum 150 boxes per view.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Datasets}

\textbf{UIEB}~\cite{liUnderwaterImageEnhancement2019} comprises 890 paired underwater images. We employ its 90-image benchmark split at native resolution to verify published restoration metrics.

\textbf{RUOD}~\cite{fuRethinkingGeneralUnderwater2023} contains 14{,}000 underwater images (9{,}800 train, 4{,}200 test) across 10 categories: \emph{holothurian, echinus, scallop, starfish, fish, corals, diver, cuttlefish, turtle, jellyfish}. Long edges are scaled to 640\,px.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

Experiments ran on Kaggle with dual NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs, PyTorch 2.11 (CUDA 12.8), and Ultralytics 8.4.174. YOLO11n used $640 \times 640$ input resolution, batch size 32, and seed 0. ResNet18 trained for 15 epochs with AdamW (learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$) on an 80/20 train/validation split (7{,}840 train, 1{,}960 val).

\subsection{Metrics}

PSNR and SSIM are evaluated via \texttt{scikit-image}. Detection accuracy is measured by COCO mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.5:0.95 through \texttt{pycocotools}. Reported confidence intervals represent paired bootstrap 95\% intervals over 1{,}000 iterations.

\subsubsection{Uniform Enhancement}
Applying SCNet uniformly lowers mAP@0.5:0.95 by 0.0026 ($-0.0031$ per image, CI $[-0.0049, -0.0013]$). Semi-UIR lowers mAP by 0.0071 ($-0.0056$ per image, CI $[-0.0077, -0.0034]$). Both confidence intervals lie below zero ($p_{\text{boot}} = 1.000$). Random selection drops accuracy by 0.0038 per image.

\subsubsection{Selector Accuracy}
Selectors lacking a threshold (\texttt{ce} and \texttt{reg}) lose detection accuracy, reflecting unnecessary enhancement calls. Adding decision threshold $\tau = 0.625$ allows Selector \texttt{ce+$\tau$} to reach 0.6159 mAP@0.5:0.95 (+3.2\% gap closed), comparable to the 0.6155 baseline. The 95\% CI of its per-image difference, $[-0.0010, +0.0003]$, spans zero ($p_{\text{boot}} = 0.851$).

\subsubsection{Confusion Matrix}
Table~\ref{tab:confusion} presents validation metrics and the test confusion matrix. Overall test accuracy is 56.9\%, trailing the 62.4\% obtainable by defaulting to raw images. Under the labeling margin $\delta = 0.01$, SCNet was the winning candidate on 846 test images (20.1\% of inputs); the selector selected SCNet on 83 of these cases (9.8\% recall). Semi-UIR won on 734 test images (17.5\%); the selector picked it on 48 instances (6.5\% recall). When unconstrained by $\delta$, pairwise oracles show that SCNet exceeds raw inputs on 31.6\% of images (1{,}327 frames), and Semi-UIR on 31.7\% (1{,}331 frames).

The selector matches the raw baseline primarily because threshold $\tau = 0.625$ leaves inputs unenhanced for 85.5\% of test images (3{,}593 cases). Overhead is 7.7\,ms per frame.

\subsubsection{Per-Class Results}
Table~\ref{tab:perclass} lists per-class AP across all 10 categories. For \emph{holothurian}, \emph{echinus}, and \emph{scallop}, the selector remains close to raw baselines. The largest variation occurs on \emph{jellyfish} (0.6089 versus 0.6026), an isolated class effect not subjected to separate confidence bounds.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We explored selective underwater image enhancement for object detection using YOLO11n on the RUOD benchmark.

Applying SCNet or Semi-UIR across all inputs reduced mAP@0.5:0.95 by 0.0026 and 0.0071 relative to raw inputs. A ResNet18 selector (7.7\,ms latency) with threshold $\tau = 0.625$ matched raw baseline mAP (0.6159 versus 0.6155), retaining raw inputs for 85.5\% of images.

An oracle choosing between raw frames and their horizontal reflections gained +0.0207 per-image mAP, reaching 144\% of the SCNet optical oracle gain (+0.0144). This indicates that detector sensitivity to spatial alterations accounts for an appreciable fraction of the apparent oracle gap. Four-view WBF yielded +0.0023 mAP over the full test set (95\% CI $[+0.0014, +0.0051]$).

Multi-version training elevated raw-frame mAP@0.5:0.95 from 0.5990 to 0.6155 over 50-epoch raw baselines and matched compute-matched 150-epoch raw detectors (0.6150). It further outperformed raw detectors across 12 unseen degradation conditions, retaining 68.5\% of clean mAP versus 65.0\%. In our experiments, enhanced imagery yielded its primary benefit as multi-domain training data for environmental robustness rather than as a per-image test-time choice.
